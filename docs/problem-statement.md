# Problem Statement

## Who is affected?
**Corporate employees, Tier-1 IT helpdesk personnel, and non-technical staff** across organizations serve as the primary targets of social engineering attacks.

## What exactly is going wrong?
Human error remains the primary vulnerability exploited by cybercriminals through psychological manipulation tactics—such as artificial urgency, panic, and fake authority—across email, smishing, and vishing channels, contributing to **60% to 68% of all organizational data breaches globally**. Conventional cybersecurity awareness training relies on passive learning formats (e.g., static slides, annual compliance videos, and mandatory quizzes) that fail to engage users, creating a critical **"awareness-action gap"** where employees cannot translate theoretical security rules into practical threat detection habits under real-world pressure.

## Why does it matter?
Social engineering is the leading mechanism behind organizational cyber theft, generating severe financial costs with global data breach costs averaging **\$4.88 million per incident** alongside critical operational downtime, loss of sensitive data, and long-term reputational damage.

## Problem statement (3 to 5 sentences)
Corporate employees and non-technical personnel remain highly vulnerable to sophisticated social engineering attacks that exploit cognitive biases through tactics like artificial urgency, panic, and fake authority. As a result, human error contributes to **60% to 68% of global data breaches**, incurring average breach costs exceeding \$4.88 million alongside significant operational disruptions. Traditional cybersecurity training programs rely on passive slides and annual compliance videos, creating an **"awareness-action gap"** that fails to equip users with practical decision-making skills under live pressure. Without immersive, scenario-based learning—such as a narrative-driven helpdesk simulation governed by an **Event-Driven Finite State Machine (FSM)** organizations remain unable to foster the active threat detection habits necessary to prevent costly compromises.
